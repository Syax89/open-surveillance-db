# Retention schedule (operational)

- **Status:** in force — personal open-source project, 2026-08-08 (ADR 0004)
- **Owner:** Simone Rondina (project owner / privacy contact)
- **Community-driven model applied (2026-08-05, ADR 0021):** records publish immediately from verified accounts and stay public while the community keeps confirming them — **no time-based retention for published records** (ADR 0021 § 9.3) and **no state transition ever happens on a timer** (§ 2.2). The pre-pivot moderation lifecycle and the time-based renewal review cycle (decision 2026-07-31, ADR 0008) are **retired** as a state machine; the record statuses are `active` / `hidden` / `removed` (+ `demo` technical marker). Two **legacy states still get a timer-based hard delete** and are documented below: `pending` submissions never published (R19) and legacy `rejected` records (R20). Privacy contact `privacy@opensurveillancedb.org` (dedicated, monitored mailbox) unchanged.
- **Photo evidence retention (2026-08-01, privacy/legal — audit gap G6; updated 2026-08-05, ADR 0021; **RETIRED 2026-08-08, CEO decision):** the image-evidence feature was **removed entirely** — no new image data is collected, the `photos` table was dropped (migration `0043`) and the retention sweep no longer touches the bucket. Existing R2 `PHOTOS` objects are **retained without deletion**. The retired photo-level lifecycle (R13) is documented below as history.
- **Community data retention (2026-08-01, DPO — community system; rewritten 2026-08-05 for ADR 0021):** rule **R14** for **community actions** (ADR 0021 § 3/§ 13) — actions follow the account (active → atomic deletion at erasure); the public lifecycle history holds aggregates only and survives erasure; audit entries keep the 2-year rule (R5). The pre-pivot community plan (COMMUNITY_PLAN.md § 5) is **not implemented** — no public profile, no attribution; contributors edit only their own records through the owner-only edit flow (`/records/[id]/edit`, two-track PATCH, ADR 0018/0021).
- **Authentication-method retention (2026-08-02, ADR 0020 — AUTH MULTI-METODO):** new rule **R15** for email-verification tokens, passkey credentials and recovery codes — verification tokens 24 h, reset tokens 3 h, both single-use; passkeys/recovery codes while the account is active, hard-deleted at erasure (R7); `external_sub` cleared so no provider/device link survives.
- **Failed-login counter retention (2026-08-03, security audit — AUDIT_REPORT finding 5, review P3-10):** new rule **R16** for `login_attempts` rows (ADR 0016 lockout counters) — **30 days** after the last failed attempt (`window_start`), hard-deleted by the retention cron with a bounded sweep; rows under an **active lock** (`locked_until` in the future) are never touched.
- **Auth-cap state retention (2026-08-03, security audit):** new rules **R17** (`registrations_ip_log`, **30 days**) and **R18** (`email_send_log`, **24 hours**) — the per-IP registration cap and the mail budget only count rows inside their windows; the older rows are dead state that accumulated forever (and unsalted SHA-256 caller keys violate data minimisation), now swept by the retention cron.
- **Demo-record purge enforcement (2026-08-04, QA finding B):** R12 is now **enforced by the retention cron** — every sweep outside `ENVIRONMENT=development` hard-deletes `demo` records with their evidence (the fail-closed public gate only hid them; the only DELETE was none). The mail-log side of the QA finding A is already covered by R18 (24 h).
- **API-key retention (2026-08-09, ADR 0023 — write API keys):** new rule **R21** for `api_keys` rows (SHA-256 key hash, 10-character display prefix, scope list, created/expiry/revocation timestamps, throttled last-used) — **hash + metadata kept while the account is active**; rows whose `revoked_at` / `expires_at` has passed are **hard-deleted 90 days** after revocation/expiry (enforcement lands with the `api_keys` backend, ADR 0023); **account erasure hard-deletes all keys of the account** in the same atomic batch (art. 17, `eraseContributor`); backups hold hashes only (no raw key material ever at rest).
- **Legal basis:** GDPR art. 5(1)(e) (storage limitation), art. 17 (erasure); D.Lgs. 196/2003 (Codice Privacy, IT) as primary jurisdiction; consistent with `../PRIVACY_AND_SAFETY.md` and `../MODERATION.md`.
- **Scope:** all data held by OpenSurveillanceDB, including reports, community actions, residual-moderation data (legal-emergency actions), record evidence (text metadata — no images, feature removed 2026-08-08), correction requests and backups. Photo data (R13) is retained from the retired feature as history.

> **Disclaimer:** this document is product guidance / not legal advice. The document is in force for the pilot jurisdiction (Italy); per-jurisdiction review remains the documented precondition for an EU-wide launch.

## 2. Erasure requests (art. 17 GDPR)

1. Receive request via `privacy@opensurveillancedb.org` (contact defined in the privacy notice).
2. Verify the requester's identity proportionately (see PRIVACY_NOTICE.md § rights).
3. Assess exceptions: art. 17(3) allows retention where necessary for the public-interest dataset purpose or legal obligations; a minimal, clearly justified retention (e.g. record provenance) may be kept — documented in the correction log.
4. Execute deletion: record (where the request concerns the contributor's own record), evidence (R6), community actions (R14), linked metadata; note that backups rotate within 30 days (R10).
5. Respond within 1 month (art. 12(3)); extendable by 2 months with notice; state the reason for any refusal and the right to complain to the supervisory authority.

**Definition of "deletion".** "Deletion" means irreversible deletion from the database and, where technically feasible, from backups. Otherwise the data is excluded from all future processing and exports. The erasure response states which of the two applies. (Retired photo objects in R2 are **not** subject to deletion — the feature was removed with a no-delete mandate, R13.)

**Legal hold.** Any ongoing litigation, complaint, or supervisory-authority inquiry suspends the relevant deletions until the matter is closed. The hold, its scope and its end date are recorded in the audit log (R4/R5).

## 3. Operational enforcement

- **The retention cron never changes record status.** Under the community model, `active`/`hidden`/`removed` transitions are driven only by community actions or legal emergencies (ADR 0021 § 2.2) — the scheduled job deletes data whose retention elapsed, it does not push records through lifecycle states. The old freshness sweep is retired.
- The retention cron enforces: **R12** (demo purge outside `ENVIRONMENT=development`), **R16** (failed-login counters), **R17** (registration-cap log), **R18** (email-send log), **R19** (`pending` records → hard delete after 90 days from submission), **R20** (`rejected` records → hard delete after 30 days from the rejection decision); **R21 is adopted (ADR 0023)** — enforcement lands with the `api_keys` backend (write-auth epic, tracked T1-T17). It also archives audit entries (R4/R5/R9) at the 2-year mark. Token TTLs (R15) are enforced inline by the auth code (single-use, on-use/expiry deletion). The retired **R13** (photo sweep) is **not enforced** — the image-evidence feature was removed 2026-08-08 and the sweep never touches the bucket. **Implemented:** `db/retention.ts` + the scheduled cron binding in `worker/index.ts` (daily 03:00 UTC) + tests.
- Audit log entries (R4/R5/R9) are archived, not hard-deleted, until the 2-year mark.
- Failed-login counters (R16) are swept by the same job: rows whose `window_start` is older than 30 days are hard-deleted in bounded batches (≤100 keys per statement, loop capped per run); rows under an active lock are exempt so the sweep never disables a lockout in progress.
- Demo records (R12) are swept by the same job on every run **outside `ENVIRONMENT=development`** (fail-closed, same gate as `demoRecordsPublic`): each `demo` camera is hard-deleted with its evidence (R6) and its queue items closed in one atomic batch; in development the illustrative seed survives.
- Legacy `pending`/`rejected` records (R19/R20) are swept by the same job in one atomic batch per record (record + open queue items). Both sweeps skip a record with an **open appeal** (`moderation_appeals` status `pending`/`escalated`) or an **active legal hold** (audit event `legal-hold` with no later `legal-hold-release`) — a hard delete is irreversible, so the law-first rule is to skip and retry on a later run.
- The privacy/legal owner (data-protection contact) reviews this schedule annually and on any material change of purpose, provider, or jurisdiction.

## 4. Legal rationale

- Storage limitation (art. 5(1)(e)): every category has an explicit, justified retention — including justified **no-time-based retention** for community-maintained records (R1/R2/R3) and photo states (R13) — and a deletion path; nothing is kept "just in case" beyond the reversible-state windows that the community model requires.
- Right to erasure (art. 17): the schedule implements deletion without undue delay and documents exceptions; withdrawn states remain subject to correction (art. 16) and erasure rights at any time.
- Accountability (art. 5(2)): retention decisions are recorded in ADR 0004 and this schedule; the public lifecycle history (ADR 0021 § 7) is the transparency control of the community model.
- Italian Codice Privacy (D.Lgs. 196/2003): no additional national retention mandates apply to this dataset; if official-source records are republished, national transparency rules are checked per record (`source: official`).
