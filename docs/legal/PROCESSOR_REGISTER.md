## 2. International transfers (Cap. V GDPR) — assessment summary

### Cloudflare (PR1)
- **Transfer instrument:** Cloudflare DPA (**v6.3, June 2025**) with **EU Standard Contractual Clauses (2021/914)** — controller-to-processor and processor-to-processor modules as applicable; the DPA is incorporated in Cloudflare's Terms of Service, accepted at account setup by the controller (**Simone Rondina (syax89) / OpenSurveillanceDB**). **EU–US Data Privacy Framework:** Cloudflare is DPF-certified; the DPF is recorded as an additional transfer ground (the SCCs alone are sufficient; DPF strengthens the transfer assessment).
- **Supplementary measures (TIA summary):** data at rest in D1 pinned to `weur` (Western Europe); TLS for all traffic; Workers edge execution minimises data transfer (dynamic app, not bulk data); Cloudflare's published sub-processor list and incident-notification commitments accepted in the DPA. The retired R2 photo storage (PR #64) no longer receives writes — the retained objects are covered by the same Cloudflare DPA; no new transfer assessment needed (no new photo data).
- **Residual risk:** low for the dataset described (mostly non-personal infrastructure data + pseudonymous contributor metadata). Reassessed annually.

### OpenAI (PR2)
- The sign-in is initiated by the moderator on OpenAI's service; identity attributes are then delivered to us. Our processing of those attributes is EU-side; we do not transfer them onward. The exposure is limited to the authentication moment; mitigated by: never logging emails, session-only use, reviewer pseudonyms in audit logs (M4).

### GitHub / Google as OIDC identity providers (PR5/PR6 — conditional)
- **Dormant by default.** While OIDC is not activated, no login data flows to GitHub/Google (PR5/PR6 are not processors). At activation (AUTH MULTI-METODO Fase D), the exchange is limited to the authentication moment: the provider authenticates the contributor and we receive only the subject id and the verified flag — **no email, no onward transfer of OSDB data**. The transfer assessment at that point: US-based providers, covered by their DPA (SCCs) and by **EU–US Data Privacy Framework certification**, verified and recorded before activation; the residual privacy cost is the provider's own observation of the login event and the caller's IP, disclosed in the login UI risk matrix (Fase E2) and PRIVACY_NOTICE § 3.1/§ 5/§ 6.

## 3. Safeguards and commitments

- **No email logging:** application logs and audit logs must never contain `oai-authenticated-user-email` values (M4; enforced in code — follow-up for ada with H1). **Same rule for OIDC:** no provider email is ever imported (PR5/PR6), so there is nothing to log.
- **Onboarding rule:** no new processor/sub-processor may be onboarded without a DPA review and privacy/legal owner sign-off (art. 28(2)). **OIDC activation gate (ADR 0020):** GitHub/Google (PR5/PR6) move from *conditional* to *active* only after their DPA + EU–US DPF certification are verified and recorded, and the register row is flipped — never before.
- **Data residency:** D1 `location_hint = "weur"` before any real data load.
- **Photo storage (retired):** the photo upload feature was removed 2026-08-08 (CEO). The R2 `PHOTOS` binding is gone from `wrangler.jsonc`; existing objects are retained without deletion and no new writes occur — the old storage-hygiene and deletion obligations no longer apply to new data (see PR1).
- **Backup retention:** D1 automatic backups (hourly, 24 h) and Time Travel PITR (30 days) are accepted as the technical erasure horizon (RETENTION_SCHEDULE.md R10); no long-term export backups of D1 are configured.
- **Breach notification:** Cloudflare's DPA commits the processor to notifying us per art. 33(2) without undue delay; our procedure is BREACH_PROCEDURE.md. OpenAI's sign-in incidents are governed by OpenAI's own incident commitments — no OSDB data at risk there.
